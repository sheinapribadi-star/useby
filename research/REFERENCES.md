# UseBy — competitive & design references

## Products studied
| Product | What it does well | What we’ll take / avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **SuperCook** | Ingredient → recipe matching; voice pantry add; “what can I make now” | Take: pantry-first recipe discovery. Avoid: no expiration urgency, noisy recipe firehose. |
| **Samsung Food / Whisk** | Food List + use-by alerts; Vision AI photo add; prioritize near-expiry in meal plans | Take: photo → inventory; expiry-aware ranking; “search with your food list”. Avoid: heavy enterprise chrome. |
| **Family Hub / FridgeCam** | “View inside” mental model; expiration on items | Take: fridge-as-dashboard. Avoid: hardware dependency. |
| **Paprika / Recipy / FridgeFox** (user discourse) | Saved recipes + pantry coverage; expiry alerts | Take: coverage % (“you have 4/6”); editable dates. Avoid: recipe-import complexity for MVP. |
| **Too Good To Go** (adjacent) | Urgency + waste narrative, warm consumer voice | Take: waste-saved emotional payoff, not guilt. |

## Jobs to be done
1. “Something in my fridge is about to die — what should I cook *tonight*?”
2. “I’m learning to cook — give me one calm beginner path, not 11M recipes.”
3. “I don’t want to type my whole fridge — let me scan / sample fast.”

## UX principles we’ll steal
- **Urgency before novelty** (Samsung Food meal plans).
- **Explain the rank** (“why this now”) — transparency builds trust when vision is mock.
- **Editable estimates** — shelf life is a starting point, never a lecture.
- **Beginner coaching inline** — tips tied to the dish, not a blog dump.
- **Mobile-first kitchen posture** — big tap targets, one primary CTA, works with greasy thumbs.
